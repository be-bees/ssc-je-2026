## 5. Topic cards

The practice questions are written in past-paper style. They are not claimed to be actual past-paper questions.

### Thermodynamics [A] · 50 min

**Subtopics:** laws · processes (isobaric, isochoric, isothermal, adiabatic, polytropic) · Carnot cycle · entropy · pure substance and steam · Rankine · Brayton

**Concepts**
- The **zeroth law** is the basis of temperature measurement. The **first law** says energy is conserved: Q = ΔU + W.
- **Second law:** Kelvin-Planck says no engine converts all heat into work. Clausius says heat does not flow from cold to hot on its own. A machine that breaks it is a PMM-2.
- **Carnot** is the most efficient engine between two temperatures. Its efficiency depends only on those temperatures, in **Kelvin**.
- **Entropy** rises in every real process. A reversible adiabatic process is isentropic.
- **Throttling** keeps enthalpy constant. **Free expansion:** Q = 0, W = 0, ΔU = 0.
- **Steam:** the triple point is 0.01 °C. The critical point is 374.15 °C and 221.2 bar. Dryness fraction x = vapour mass / total mass. The Mollier chart plots h against s.
- Rankine efficiency rises with higher boiler pressure, superheating, lower condenser pressure, reheat and regeneration.

**Question patterns:** Carnot efficiency or COP · ΔU from Q and W · "which process has constant ___" · law statements · ways to raise Rankine efficiency

**Example 1.** Carnot engine between 527 °C and 27 °C. T1 = 800 K and T2 = 300 K, so η = 1 − 300/800 = **62.5%**. Using °C gives a wrong 94.9%.

**Example 2.** A gas gets 50 kJ of heat and does 20 kJ of work. ΔU = 50 − 20 = **+30 kJ**.

**Common mistakes:** using °C instead of K · the sign of work (work done *by* the gas is positive) · mixing up refrigerator COP T2/(T1−T2) and heat pump COP T1/(T1−T2)

### IC Engines [A] · 30 min

- **SI (petrol):** spark plug, compression ratio 6–10, Otto cycle. **CI (diesel):** compression ignition, compression ratio 14–22, Diesel cycle, fuel injector.
- **4-stroke:** 1 power stroke per 2 revolutions. **2-stroke:** 1 per revolution and needs scavenging.
- **Octane number** rates petrol anti-knock quality (iso-octane vs n-heptane). **Cetane number** rates diesel ignition quality (cetane vs α-methylnaphthalene).
- **SI knock** happens at the end of combustion. **CI knock** happens at the start, from ignition delay. The factors that reduce one increase the other.
- 4-cylinder firing order is **1-3-4-2**. The Morse test finds IP of a multi-cylinder engine. Willan's line method finds friction power in a CI engine.
- At the same compression ratio: Otto > Dual > Diesel. At the same maximum pressure: Diesel > Dual > Otto.

**Example 1.** Bore 100 mm, stroke 120 mm, clearance 157 cc. Vs = π/4 × 10² × 12 = 942 cc, so r = (942 + 157)/157 = **7**.

**Example 2.** Otto cycle, r = 8: η = 1 − 1/8^0.4 = **56.5%**.

**Common mistakes:** a 4-stroke engine has N/2 power strokes per minute · comparing cycles without checking "same r" or "same max pressure"

### Fluid Mechanics [A] · 50 min

- **Viscosity:** τ = μ du/dy. Liquid viscosity falls as temperature rises; gas viscosity rises.
- **Pressure** = ρgh. Absolute = gauge + atmospheric. 1 atm = 101.325 kPa = 10.33 m of water = 760 mm of Hg.
- A **piezometer** cannot measure gas or negative pressure. A **U-tube** manometer can. An **inverted U-tube** measures small differences.
- The **centre of pressure** is always below the centroid. A floating body is stable if GM > 0.
- **Bernoulli** assumes steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow along a streamline.
- **Venturimeter** Cd is 0.95–0.98. **Orifice meter** Cd is about 0.6–0.65. A **pitot tube** measures point velocity.
- **Reynolds number:** below 2000 is laminar, above 4000 turbulent. In laminar flow f = 64/Re and Vmax = 2 × Vavg.

**Example 1.** A pipe narrows from 200 mm to 100 mm with V1 = 2 m/s. The area falls to 1/4, so V2 = **8 m/s**.

**Example 2.** V = 0.5 m/s, D = 20 mm, ν = 10⁻⁶ m²/s. Re = 10,000, so the flow is **turbulent**.

**Common mistakes:** ν = μ/ρ · Darcy f = 4 × friction coefficient f′ · using diameter vs radius in surface-tension formulas

### Hydraulic Machines [A] · 30 min

- **Pelton:** impulse turbine for high head and low Ns, with tangential flow. Best bucket speed is half the jet speed.
- **Francis:** reaction turbine for medium head, with radial/mixed flow.
- **Kaplan:** reaction turbine for low head and high Ns, with axial flow and adjustable blades.
- A **draft tube** is used only with reaction turbines.
- **Specific speed:** turbine N√P / H^(5/4); pump N√Q / H^(3/4).
- **Centrifugal pump:** needs priming and uses backward-curved vanes. Avoid cavitation by keeping NPSHa > NPSHr. Pumps in series add head; in parallel they add discharge.
- **Reciprocating pump:** an air vessel smooths the flow. Negative slip means actual discharge > theoretical.

**Example.** Pelton jet at 40 m/s: best bucket speed u = V/2 = **20 m/s**.

### Strength of Materials [A] · 60 min

- The stress-strain curve runs: proportional limit → elastic limit → yield → ultimate → fracture.
- Poisson's ratio μ is 0–0.5. It is about 0.3 for steel, near 0 for cork and near 0.5 for rubber.
- A **suddenly applied load** causes twice the stress of a gradual one.
- Bending stress is 0 at the neutral axis and maximum at the outer fibres. Shear stress is maximum at the neutral axis.
- Where shear force is 0, bending moment is maximum.
- A hollow shaft is stronger than a solid shaft of the same weight.
- **Euler effective length:** hinged-hinged L, fixed-fixed L/2, fixed-free 2L, fixed-hinged L/√2.

**Example 1.** E = 200 GPa and μ = 0.25. G = 200/2.5 = **80 GPa**.

**Example 2.** SSB of 4 m span with 10 kN at the centre. M = WL/4 = **10 kN·m**.

**Common mistakes:** torsion uses J = πd⁴/32, not πd⁴/64 · 1 MPa = 1 N/mm² · a fixed-fixed column carries 4 × the hinged-hinged load and a fixed-free one carries 1/4

### Engineering Mechanics [A] · 25 min

- F = μN. The angle of repose equals the angle of friction (tan φ = μ). Static friction > kinetic friction. Friction does not depend on contact area.
- **Lami's theorem:** for 3 concurrent forces in equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two.
- Projectile range is maximum at 45°. θ and (90° − θ) give the same range.
- Centroids: triangle h/3 from its base, semicircle 4r/3π from its diameter.

**Example 1.** 20 m/s at 45°, g = 10. R = 400/10 = **40 m**.

**Example 2.** A block starts sliding at 30°. μ = tan 30° = **0.577**.

### Production / Manufacturing [A] · 70 min

**Casting**
- Pattern allowances: shrinkage, draft, machining, distortion, shake. Shake is the only **negative** one.
- The riser must solidify **last**. Chvorinov's rule: solidification time ∝ (V/A)².
- Defects: blow holes (gas), cold shut and misrun (low fluidity), hot tears (restrained contraction).
- Investment casting = lost wax. Centrifugal casting makes pipes. Die casting is for non-ferrous metals.

**Welding**
- Neutral flame O₂ : C₂H₂ = 1:1, about 3200 °C. Oxidizing flame (extra O₂) for brass and bronze. Carburizing flame (extra acetylene) for high-carbon steel.
- TIG uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode. MIG uses a consumable wire. Spot and seam welding are resistance welding. Thermit welding is used for rails.
- Brazing is above 450 °C, soldering below. Neither melts the base metal.

**Machining**
- V = πDN/1000 (m/min). Taylor: VTⁿ = C, with n about 0.1 for HSS, 0.25 for carbide and 0.5 for ceramics.
- Continuous chips: ductile material at high speed. Discontinuous: brittle material. Built-up edge: low speed on ductile material.
- Merchant: φ = 45° + α/2 − β/2.
- Grinding: Al₂O₃ for steel, SiC for cast iron and non-ferrous. Specification order: abrasive, grain size, grade, structure, bond.
- Down milling = climb milling. A shaper uses a quick-return mechanism.
- EDM: conductive workpiece + dielectric. ECM: Faraday's law. USM: hard, brittle materials. EBM: needs a vacuum. LBM: any material.

**Forming**
- Hot working is above the recrystallization temperature; cold working is below.
- **Punching:** the punch is the hole size and clearance goes on the die. **Blanking:** the die is the blank size and clearance goes on the punch. Shearing force F = L × t × τ.

**Example 1.** D = 50 mm at 600 rpm. V = π × 50 × 600/1000 = **94.2 m/min**.

**Example 2.** n = 0.5 and the speed doubles. T₂/T₁ = (1/2)² = **1/4**.

### Engineering Materials [A] · 30 min

- Steel has up to about 2% C; cast iron has 2–6.67% C. **Eutectoid:** 0.8% C at 727 °C (pearlite). **Eutectic:** 4.3% C at 1147 °C (ledeburite).
- Low carbon steel < 0.3% C, medium 0.3–0.6%, high 0.6–1.5%. 40C8 = 0.40% C and 0.8% Mn. Stainless steel has ≥ 10.5–12% Cr; 18-8 = 18% Cr, 8% Ni. HSS 18-4-1 = 18% W, 4% Cr, 1% V.
- Annealing (furnace cool) softens. Normalizing (air cool) refines grain. Hardening (quench) gives martensite. Tempering reduces brittleness. Case hardening includes carburizing, nitriding and cyaniding.
- **BCC** (α-Fe, Cr, W, Mo): APF 0.68, 2 atoms. **FCC** (γ-Fe, Al, Cu, Ni, Ag, Au, Pb): APF 0.74, 4 atoms. **HCP** (Zn, Mg, Ti): APF 0.74, 6 atoms.
- Brass = Cu + Zn. Bronze = Cu + Sn. Gun metal = Cu + Sn + Zn. Duralumin = Al + Cu + Mg + Mn. Invar = Fe + 36% Ni. German silver = Cu + Zn + Ni.
- Brinell uses a steel ball, Vickers a diamond pyramid, Rockwell a cone (C) or ball (B). Charpy and Izod are impact tests.

**Example.** 0.4% C steel cooled slowly is hypoeutectoid: **ferrite + pearlite**.

### Theory of Machines [B] · 35 min

- Kutzbach: n = 3(L − 1) − 2j − h. Grashof: s + l ≤ p + q.
- Single slider-crank inversions: Whitworth and crank-and-slotted-lever (quick return), rotary engine. Double slider-crank inversions: elliptical trammel, Scotch yoke, Oldham coupling.
- Belt: N₂/N₁ = d₁/d₂ and T₁/T₂ = e^(μθ). Maximum power when Tc = Tmax/3.
- Module m = D/T. Spur gears: parallel shafts. Bevel: intersecting. Worm: perpendicular non-intersecting, high ratio, can self-lock.
- Watt governor h = 895/N² m. Porter and Proell are dead-weight types; Hartnell is spring-loaded. Isochronous = zero speed range. Hunting = too sensitive.
- A flywheel limits speed changes within a cycle. A governor controls mean speed.
- ωn = √(k/m). Springs in series: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂. In parallel: k₁ + k₂.

### Boilers, Nozzles, Turbines, Compressors [A] · 35 min

- Fire-tube: Cochran, Lancashire, Cornish, Locomotive. Water-tube: Babcock & Wilcox, Stirling. High-pressure: Lamont, Benson (drumless), Loeffler, Velox.
- **Mountings** (safety): safety valve, water-level indicator, pressure gauge, fusible plug, stop valve, blow-off cock, feed check valve. **Accessories** (efficiency): economiser, superheater, air preheater, feed pump, injector.
- Critical pressure ratio: air 0.528, superheated steam 0.546, saturated steam 0.577.
- De Laval = impulse turbine. Parsons = reaction turbine, 50% reaction. Compounding reduces rotor speed.
- Isothermal compression needs the least work. Multistage with intercooling: p₂ = √(p₁p₃). Volumetric efficiency falls as clearance and pressure ratio rise.

### Refrigeration & AC [A] · 30 min

- 1 TR = 3.5 kW = 210 kJ/min. COP = refrigerating effect / work.
- VCR cycle: compressor → condenser → expansion valve (isenthalpic) → evaporator.

## 7. Reasoning shortcuts

Do these first; they are fast and appear in nearly every paper. Leave long puzzles for the end.

- **Number series:** check differences → second differences → ×/÷ → squares/cubes ± → alternate terms. Example: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? → **42**.
- **Alphabet:** A = 1 … Z = 26. EJOTY = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25. Opposite letter = 27 − n (D ↔ W).
- **Coding:** find the shift (+1, −1, reverse, opposite). CAT → DBU, so DOG → **EPH**.
- **Mathematical operations:** rewrite with the swapped symbols first, then apply BODMAS.
- **Ranking:** total = left + right − 1. 12th + 18th gives **29**.
- **Direction:** draw it. Distance = √(x² + y²). Morning shadow falls west; evening shadow falls east.
- **Blood relation:** draw a tree. "My mother's only son" = the speaker's brother (or the speaker, if male).
- **Clock angle:** θ = |30H − 5.5M|. At 3:30 it is **75°**. The hands overlap 22 times a day.
- **Calendar:** normal year +1 day, leap year +2. A century year is leap only if divisible by 400.
- **Syllogism:** draw the smallest Venn diagram. "All A are B" + "Some B are C" does not give "Some A are C". "All A are B" always gives "Some B are A".
- **Mirror image:** left ↔ right. **Water image:** top ↔ bottom.
- **Dice:** faces adjacent to the same face can't be opposite. On a standard die, opposite faces add to 7.
- **Missing number:** try row sum, row product, then (a × b) + c.
- **Dictionary order:** compare letter by letter.

## 11. Exam strategy (120 minutes)

| Round | Section | Minutes | Rule |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | General Awareness | 10 | Answer only what you know instantly. No more than 15 seconds per question. |
| 2 | Mechanical, first pass | 55 | Theory and one-step formula questions. Mark any calculation longer than 90 seconds. |
| 3 | Reasoning | 35 | Series, coding, ranking, clock, calendar first. Puzzles last. |
| 4 | Marked questions | 20 | Mechanical numericals first, then reasoning puzzles. |

**Negative marking, with the numbers**
- A blind guess among 4 options is worth +0.06 marks on average. With one option ruled out: +0.17. With two ruled out: +0.38.
- Guess only after ruling out at least one option. Keep blind guesses to 10 or fewer, at the very end.
- Target: 150 attempted at 85% accuracy is 127.5 right and 22.5 wrong, giving ≈ **122 marks**.
- If two options both look right, check the trap line for that topic.

**Skip a question when** it needs a steam-table lookup (the table is on screen under "Useful Data", but leave those for round 4), when 90 seconds have passed without seeing the formula, or when it is a Category C topic and not one line long.

**Avoiding calculation mistakes**
- **[Confirmed]** A scientific calculator is on screen for the whole exam. Use it for roots, powers like r^0.4, logs and long multiplications. Clicking it with the mouse is slow, so do simple arithmetic in your head.
- **[Confirmed]** Rough-work pen and paper are given in the exam lab, and a timer runs on screen.
- Convert units first: K, m and N (or mm, N and MPa), radians.
- Estimate the size of the answer first. Options 10 × apart point to a units error; 2 × or 4 × apart point to a ratio error.
- Use the rough sheet in numbered boxes so you can recheck.
- Before you click, re-read the last line: diameter or radius? Gauge or absolute? Maximum or average?

## 12. Exam-day checklist

From your admit card and SSC's general instructions (notice of 21 Jul 2025). [Confirmed]

- [ ] Printed admit card. **Do not write anything on it before the exam**, or you will not be let in. Do not fold or damage the barcode.
- [ ] One **original** photo ID with the **same date of birth** as the admit card (Aadhaar or e-Aadhaar printout, PAN, voter ID, driving licence, passport, college ID).
- [ ] Reach by **3:45 PM**. **Entry closes 4:45 PM** and no one is let in after that.
- [ ] Allowed: a **transparent** water bottle. Not allowed: watch of any kind, phone, Bluetooth/earphones, calculator, pen, paper, books. Leave your bag at home if you can; the centre does not store it.
- [ ] Avoid rings, bracelets, chains and other jewellery; there is metal-detector and hand frisking.
- [ ] Face uncovered (no mask). Sit so the webcam sees your full face the whole time; photos are taken during the exam.
- [ ] No toilet break in a 2-hour exam. Eat and use the toilet before you go in.
- [ ] After the first login, write the certification statement in your own running handwriting, sign and give your left thumb impression on the admit card, in front of the invigilator. Also write your mother's name there.
- [ ] At the computer: check your name and photo, find the **calculator icon** and the **"Useful Data" steam table**, and learn the question palette colours.
- [ ] In the last 2 minutes, check that nothing is "marked" without an answer you mean to give. Check how your shift's on-screen instructions treat "marked for review" answers.
- [ ] Get the invigilator to sign your admit card before you leave, and keep a copy of it.
- [ ] Plan your ride home; the exam ends at 7 PM.
