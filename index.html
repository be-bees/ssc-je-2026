Thermodynamics A

Subtopics

Laws of thermodynamics · processes (isobaric, isochoric, isothermal, adiabatic, polytropic) · Carnot cycle · entropy · pure substance and steam · Rankine cycle · Brayton cycle

Concepts in plain English

  • Zeroth law is the basis of temperature measurement. First law says energy is conserved: Q = ΔU + W (heat in = rise in internal energy + work out).
  • Second law: Kelvin-Planck says no engine converts all heat into work. Clausius says heat does not flow from cold to hot on its own. A machine that breaks it is a PMM-2.
  • Carnot is the most efficient engine between two temperatures. Its efficiency depends only on those temperatures, in Kelvin.
  • Entropy is a property. It rises in every real (irreversible) process. A reversible adiabatic process is isentropic (constant entropy).
  • Throttling keeps enthalpy constant. Free expansion: Q = 0, W = 0, ΔU = 0.
  • Steam: triple point of water is 0.01 °C. The critical point is 374.15 °C and 221.2 bar. Dryness fraction x = mass of vapour / total mass. The Mollier chart plots h against s.
  • Rankine efficiency rises with higher boiler pressure, superheating, lower condenser pressure, reheat and regeneration.

Question patterns

Carnot efficiency or COP from temperatures · ΔU from Q and W · "which process has constant ___" · statement of a law · ways to raise Rankine efficiency

Example 1. A Carnot engine works between 527 °C and 27 °C. Find its efficiency.

T1 = 800 K, T2 = 300 K. η = 1 − 300/800 = 62.5%. (Using °C gives a wrong 94.9%.)

Example 2. A gas receives 50 kJ of heat and does 20 kJ of work. Find the change in internal energy.

ΔU = Q − W = 50 − 20 = +30 kJ.

Common mistakes

  • Using °C instead of K.
  • Sign of work: work done by the gas is positive.
  • Mixing up the refrigerator COP (T2/(T1−T2)) and the heat pump COP (T1/(T1−T2)).

Time: 50 min

IC Engines A

Subtopics

Otto, Diesel and Dual cycles · 2-stroke vs 4-stroke · SI vs CI · knocking · fuel ratings · performance (IP, BP, efficiencies) · cooling and lubrication

Concepts

  • SI (petrol) engines use a spark plug. Compression ratio is 6–10, the cycle is Otto and fuel is controlled by a carburettor or injector.
  • CI (diesel) engines use compression ignition. Compression ratio is 14–22, the cycle is Diesel, with a fuel injector.
  • A 4-stroke engine gives one power stroke every 2 crank revolutions. A 2-stroke engine gives one every revolution and needs scavenging.
  • Octane number rates petrol (SI) anti-knock quality, using iso-octane and n-heptane. Cetane number rates diesel (CI) ignition quality, using cetane and α-methylnaphthalene.
  • SI knock happens at the end of combustion, when the end-gas self-ignites. CI knock happens at the start, from a long ignition delay. The factors that reduce SI knock increase CI knock, and the reverse.
  • Firing order of a 4-cylinder inline engine: 1-3-4-2. The Morse test finds the IP of a multi-cylinder engine. Willan's line method finds friction power in a CI engine.
  • For the same compression ratio: η Otto > Dual > Diesel. For the same maximum pressure: Diesel > Dual > Otto.

Question patterns

Otto efficiency from r · swept volume and compression ratio · octane vs cetane · which engine part/process · comparing cycles

Example 1. Bore 100 mm, stroke 120 mm, clearance volume 157 cc. Find the compression ratio.

Vs = π/4 × 10² × 12 = 942 cc. r = (942 + 157)/157 = 7.

Example 2. Otto cycle with r = 8 and γ = 1.4.

η = 1 − 1/8^0.4 = 1 − 1/2.297 = 56.5%.

Common mistakes

  • For a 4-stroke engine, power strokes per minute are N/2, not N.
  • Comparing Otto and Diesel without checking "same r" or "same max pressure".

Time: 30 min

Fluid Mechanics A

Subtopics

Fluid properties · pressure and its measurement · hydrostatic forces and buoyancy · kinematics (continuity) · Bernoulli · flow measurement · laminar/turbulent flow · pipe losses · notches

Concepts

  • Viscosity is a fluid's resistance to flow. Newton's law: τ = μ du/dy. For liquids, viscosity falls as temperature rises. For gases it rises.
  • Pressure at depth h is ρgh. Absolute pressure = gauge + atmospheric. 1 atm = 101.325 kPa = 10.33 m of water = 760 mm of Hg.
  • A piezometer cannot measure gas or negative pressure. A U-tube manometer can. An inverted U-tube measures small differences.
  • The centre of pressure on a submerged vertical surface is always below the centroid. A floating body is stable if the metacentre lies above the centre of gravity (GM > 0).
  • Bernoulli assumes steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow along a streamline. Pressure head + velocity head + datum head = constant.
  • A venturimeter has Cd 0.95–0.98. An orifice meter has Cd about 0.6–0.65. A pitot tube measures velocity at a point.
  • Reynolds number in a pipe: below 2000 is laminar, above 4000 is turbulent. In laminar flow, f = 64/Re and Vmax = 2 × Vavg.

Question patterns

Pressure at depth · velocity after a change in diameter · flow regime from Re · which device measures what · Bernoulli assumptions · surface tension formulas

Example 1. Water flows in a pipe that narrows from 200 mm to 100 mm. V1 = 2 m/s. Find V2.

A ∝ d². The area falls to 1/4, so V2 = 4 × 2 = 8 m/s.

Example 2. V = 0.5 m/s, D = 20 mm, ν = 1×10⁻⁶ m²/s. What is the flow regime?

Re = VD/ν = 0.5 × 0.02 / 10⁻⁶ = 10,000, so the flow is turbulent.

Common mistakes

  • Kinematic (ν) vs dynamic (μ) viscosity: ν = μ/ρ.
  • Darcy f vs the friction coefficient f′: f = 4f′.
  • Using diameter as if it were radius in surface-tension formulas.

Time: 50 min

Hydraulic Machines A

Concepts

  • Pelton wheel: impulse turbine for high head and low specific speed, with tangential flow. Best bucket speed is half the jet speed.
  • Francis turbine: reaction turbine for medium head, with inward radial/mixed flow.
  • Kaplan turbine: reaction turbine for low head and high specific speed, with axial flow and adjustable blades. A propeller turbine has fixed blades.
  • A draft tube is used only with reaction turbines. It recovers kinetic energy and lets the turbine sit above the tailrace.
  • Specific speed for a turbine is N√P / H^(5/4). For a pump it is N√Q / H^(3/4).
  • Centrifugal pump: needs priming and uses backward-curved vanes. Avoid cavitation by keeping NPSH available above NPSH required. Pumps in series add head; pumps in parallel add discharge.
  • Reciprocating pump: an air vessel smooths the flow and saves work. Negative slip means actual discharge is more than theoretical.

Example. The jet velocity of a Pelton wheel is 40 m/s. Find the bucket speed for maximum efficiency.

u = V/2 = 20 m/s.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping the exponents 5/4 (turbine) and 3/4 (pump).
  • Thinking Pelton has a draft tube. It does not.

Time: 30 min

Strength of Materials A

Subtopics

Stress, strain and Hooke's law · elastic constants · thermal stress · strain energy · SFD/BMD · bending stress · deflection · torsion · columns · thin cylinders · principal stresses

Concepts

  • Hooke's law holds up to the limit of proportionality. On the stress-strain curve the points come in order: proportional limit → elastic limit → upper/lower yield → ultimate → fracture.
  • Poisson's ratio μ is between 0 and 0.5. It is about 0.3 for steel, near 0 for cork and near 0.5 for rubber.
  • A suddenly applied load causes twice the stress of the same load applied gradually.
  • Bending stress is zero at the neutral axis and largest at the outer fibres. Shear stress is largest at the neutral axis.
  • Where the shear force is zero, the bending moment is maximum.
  • A hollow shaft is stronger than a solid shaft of the same weight.
  • Euler's formula applies to long columns. Effective length: both ends hinged L, both fixed L/2, one fixed one free 2L, one fixed one hinged L/√2.

Question patterns

Find G or K from E and μ · thermal stress · max BM or deflection for standard cases · torque and power · Euler load ratio · hoop vs longitudinal stress

Example 1. E = 200 GPa and μ = 0.25. Find G.

G = E / 2(1 + μ) = 200 / 2.5 = 80 GPa.

Example 2. A simply supported beam of 4 m span carries a 10 kN load at its centre. Find the maximum bending moment.

M = WL/4 = 10 × 4/4 = 10 kN·m.

Common mistakes

  • Taking I = πd⁴/64 for torsion. Torsion uses J = πd⁴/32.
  • Units: 1 MPa = 1 N/mm².
  • Euler ratios: a fixed-fixed column carries 4 times the load of a hinged-hinged one, and a fixed-free column carries 1/4.

Time: 60 min

Engineering Mechanics A
  • Friction: F = μN. The angle of repose equals the angle of friction, so tan φ = μ. Static friction is greater than kinetic friction. Friction does not depend on contact area.
  • Lami's theorem: applies to three concurrent forces in equilibrium. Each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two.
  • Projectile: range is maximum at 45°. Angles θ and (90° − θ) give the same range.
  • Centroids: a triangle's is h/3 from its base. A semicircle's is 4r/3π from its diameter.

Example 1. A ball is thrown at 20 m/s at 45°, with g = 10. Find the range.

R = u² sin 2θ / g = 400 × 1 / 10 = 40 m.

Example 2. A block on an incline starts to slide at 30°. Find μ.

μ = tan 30° = 0.577.

Production / Manufacturing A

Casting

  • Pattern allowances: shrinkage, draft, machining, distortion and shake. Shake is the only negative allowance.
  • Gating parts are the sprue, runner and gate. The riser must solidify last. Chvorinov's rule: solidification time ∝ (V/A)².
  • Defects: blow holes (gas), cold shut and misrun (low fluidity or temperature), hot tears (restrained contraction).
  • Investment casting is also called lost-wax casting. Centrifugal casting makes pipes. Die casting is used for non-ferrous metals.

Welding

  • An oxy-acetylene neutral flame (O₂ : C₂H₂ = 1:1) reaches about 3200 °C. An oxidizing flame has extra O₂ and is used for brass and bronze. A carburizing flame has extra acetylene and is used for high-carbon steel.
  • TIG uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode. MIG uses a consumable wire. SAW is submerged arc. Spot and seam welding are resistance welding. Thermit welding is used for rails.
  • Brazing is done above 450 °C and soldering below 450 °C. Neither melts the base metal.

Machining

  • Cutting speed V = πDN/1000 (m/min). Taylor's tool-life equation: VTⁿ = C. Typical n is about 0.1 for HSS, 0.25 for carbide and 0.5 for ceramics.
  • Continuous chips come from ductile material at high speed. Discontinuous chips come from brittle material. A built-up edge forms at low speed on ductile material.
  • Merchant's shear-angle relation: φ = 45° + α/2 − β/2.
  • Grinding wheels: Al₂O₃ for steels, SiC for cast iron and non-ferrous metals. The specification order is abrasive, grain size, grade, structure, bond.
  • Up milling is conventional; down milling is climb milling. A shaper uses a quick-return mechanism.
  • EDM needs a conductive workpiece and a dielectric fluid. ECM works on Faraday's law, like electroplating in reverse. USM is for hard, brittle materials such as glass. EBM needs a vacuum. LBM works on any material.

Forming

  • Hot working is done above the recrystallization temperature, cold working below it.
  • Punching: the punch is the hole size and clearance goes on the die. Blanking: the die is the blank size and clearance goes on the punch. Shearing force F = L × t × τ.

Example 1. Turning a 50 mm bar at 600 rpm. Find the cutting speed.

V = π × 50 × 600 / 1000 = 94.2 m/min.

Example 2. Taylor exponent n = 0.5. The cutting speed is doubled. What happens to tool life?

T₂/T₁ = (V₁/V₂)^(1/n) = (1/2)² = 1/4.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping punch and die clearance.
  • Forgetting to divide by 1000 in V = πDN/1000.

Time: 70 min

Engineering Materials A
  • Iron-carbon: steel has up to about 2% C; cast iron has 2–6.67% C. The eutectoid point is 0.8% C at 727 °C and gives pearlite. The eutectic point is 4.3% C at 1147 °C and gives ledeburite.
  • Steels: low carbon (mild) < 0.3% C, medium 0.3–0.6%, high 0.6–1.5%. 40C8 means 0.40% C and 0.8% Mn. Stainless steel has at least 10.5–12% Cr; 18-8 means 18% Cr and 8% Ni. HSS 18-4-1 means 18% W, 4% Cr and 1% V.
  • Heat treatment: annealing (furnace cool) softens. Normalizing (air cool) refines grain. Hardening (quench) gives martensite. Tempering reduces brittleness. Case hardening includes carburizing, nitriding and cyaniding.
  • Crystal structures: BCC (α-iron, Cr, W, Mo) has APF 0.68 and 2 atoms per cell. FCC (γ-iron, Al, Cu, Ni, Ag, Au, Pb) has APF 0.74 and 4 atoms. HCP (Zn, Mg, Ti) has APF 0.74 and 6 atoms.
  • Alloys: brass is Cu + Zn. Bronze is Cu + Sn. Gun metal is Cu + Sn + Zn. Duralumin is Al + Cu + Mg + Mn. Invar is Fe with 36% Ni. German silver is Cu + Zn + Ni.
  • Hardness tests: Brinell uses a steel ball. Vickers uses a diamond pyramid. Rockwell uses a diamond cone (C scale) or ball (B scale). Charpy and Izod are impact tests.

Example. A steel with 0.4% C, cooled slowly. What is its structure?

Below 0.8% C it is hypoeutectoid, so the structure is ferrite + pearlite.

Theory of Machines B
  • Kutzbach mobility: n = 3(L − 1) − 2j − h. Grashof's condition: s + l ≤ p + q, which allows at least one link to rotate fully.
  • Single slider-crank inversions include the Whitworth and crank-and-slotted-lever quick-return mechanisms and the rotary engine. Double slider-crank inversions are the elliptical trammel, Scotch yoke and Oldham coupling.
  • Belt: N₂/N₁ = d₁/d₂ and T₁/T₂ = e^(μθ). Maximum power is transmitted when centrifugal tension Tc = Tmax/3.
  • Gears: module m = D/T. Spur gears connect parallel shafts, bevel gears intersecting shafts, and worm gears non-intersecting perpendicular shafts (high speed ratio, can be self-locking).
  • Governors: Watt height h = 895/N² m. Porter and Proell are dead-weight types; Hartnell is spring-loaded. An isochronous governor has zero speed range. Hunting means the governor is too sensitive.
  • A flywheel limits speed changes within one cycle. A governor controls the mean speed under changing load.
  • Natural frequency ωn = √(k/m). Springs in series: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂. In parallel: k = k₁ + k₂.

Boilers, Nozzles, Turbines, Compressors A
  • Fire-tube boilers (hot gas inside the tubes): Cochran, Lancashire, Cornish, Locomotive. Water-tube boilers: Babcock & Wilcox and Stirling. High-pressure boilers: Lamont, Benson, Loeffler, Velox. Benson has no drum.
  • Mountings are for safety: safety valve, water-level indicator, pressure gauge, fusible plug, stop valve, blow-off cock, feed check valve. Accessories improve efficiency: economiser, superheater, air preheater, feed pump, injector.
  • Critical pressure ratio in a nozzle: 0.528 for air, 0.546 for superheated steam, 0.577 for saturated steam.
  • De Laval is an impulse turbine; pressure drops only in the nozzles. Parsons is a reaction turbine with 50% reaction. Compounding (pressure or velocity) reduces rotor speed.
  • Compressors: isothermal compression needs the least work. Multistage compression with intercooling saves work; the best intermediate pressure is √(p₁p₃). Volumetric efficiency falls as clearance and pressure ratio rise.

Refrigeration & AC A
  • 1 TR = 3.5 kW = 210 kJ/min. COP = refrigerating effect / work input.
  • The VCR cycle runs compressor → condenser → expansion valve → evaporator. The expansion valve is a throttling device, so enthalpy stays constant.
  • Refrigerant numbers: add 90 to the R-number to get the digits C, H, F. For R-12: 12 + 90 = 102 → C1 H0 F2 → CCl₂F₂. For R-22: 112 → CHClF₂. For R-134a: 224 → C₂H₂F₄. Ammonia is R-717.

  • Psychrometry terms: DBT, WBT, DPT and relative humidity. At saturation all three temperatures are equal.

Heat Transfer B
  • Conduction follows Fourier's law, convection follows Newton's law of cooling, and radiation follows the Stefan-Boltzmann law (∝ T⁴).
  • Thermal conductivity: silver > copper > aluminium. Gases conduct poorly.
  • A black body has absorptivity 1. Kirchhoff's law says emissivity equals absorptivity.
  • Critical radius of insulation is k/h for a cylinder and 2k/h for a sphere.
  • Nu = hL/kfluid. Bi = hL/ksolid; lumped analysis is valid when Bi < 0.1. Pr = μcp/k. Gr is used for natural convection.
  • A counter-flow heat exchanger is more effective than parallel flow.

Machine Design · Metrology · Industrial Engg B
  • Theories of failure: maximum principal stress (Rankine) for brittle materials. Maximum shear stress (Tresca/Guest) for ductile materials; it is conservative and gives τy = 0.5σy. Distortion energy (von Mises) is best for ductile materials and gives τy = 0.577σy.
  • Fatigue: Soderberg is the most conservative (uses yield strength). Goodman uses ultimate strength. Gerber is a parabola.
  • Riveted joints: Unwin's formula d = 6√t (mm). Joint efficiency = weakest strength / strength of the solid plate. Keys include sunk, Woodruff and Kennedy.
  • Metrology: Vernier least count = 1 MSD − 1 VSD. A micrometer reads to 0.01 mm. Fits are clearance, transition or interference. Hole-basis is the preferred system.
  • Industrial engineering: work study = method study + work measurement. PERT uses three time estimates (probabilistic). CPM uses one (deterministic). The critical path has zero float.

  • Galvanization is a zinc coating. Rusting needs both oxygen and water.
  • Ores: bauxite (Al), haematite (Fe), galena (Pb), cinnabar (Hg), chalcopyrite (Cu).
Biology
  • Vitamin deficiencies: A night blindness · B1 beriberi · B3 pellagra · B12 pernicious anaemia · C scurvy · D rickets · K poor blood clotting.
  • Iodine deficiency causes goitre. Iron deficiency causes anaemia.
  • Blood group O is the universal donor and AB the universal recipient. Red blood cells live about 120 days.
  • Largest gland: liver. Largest organ: skin. Smallest bone: stapes (ear). Humans have 46 chromosomes. The heart has 4 chambers.
  • Bacterial diseases: TB, typhoid, cholera, tetanus, diphtheria, leprosy, plague.
  • Viral diseases: AIDS, polio, measles, dengue, rabies, chickenpox, hepatitis, COVID-19.
  • Protozoan diseases: malaria (Plasmodium, spread by the female Anopheles mosquito), kala-azar, amoebic dysentery. Fungal: ringworm, athlete's foot.
  • Insulin comes from the pancreas; too little causes diabetes. Mitochondria are the cell's powerhouse. Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts.
Indian Polity
  • The Constitution was adopted on 26 Nov 1949 and came into force on 26 Jan 1950. The original text had 395 articles, 22 parts and 8 schedules; there are now 12 schedules.
  • The 42nd Amendment (1976) added "socialist", "secular" and "integrity" to the Preamble, and added the Fundamental Duties (Art 51A, Part IVA).
  • Fundamental Rights are in Part III, Art 12–35. Art 14 equality · Art 17 abolition of untouchability · Art 19 freedoms · Art 21 life and liberty · Art 21A education (86th Amendment, 2002) · Art 32 constitutional remedies.
  • Directive Principles (DPSP) are in Part IV, Art 36–51, borrowed from Ireland.
  • President Art 52 · impeachment Art 61 · Vice-President Art 63 (ex-officio chairman of the Rajya Sabha) · PM and council of ministers Art 75 · Attorney General Art 76.
  • Money bill Art 110 · budget (annual financial statement) Art 112 · Supreme Court Art 124 · CAG Art 148 · Governor Art 153 · Election Commission Art 324.
  • Emergencies: national Art 352, President's rule Art 356, financial Art 360. Amendment procedure Art 368.
  • Borrowed features: parliamentary system from the UK, Fundamental Rights and judicial review from the USA, DPSP from Ireland, concurrent list from Australia, Fundamental Duties from the USSR.
Indian History
  • Indus Valley sites: Harappa (Ravi), Mohenjo-daro (Indus), Lothal (dockyard), Dholavira, Kalibangan.
  • Buddha was born at Lumbini, attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya and gave his first sermon at Sarnath. Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara.
  • Ashoka fought the Kalinga war in 261 BCE. The Gupta period is called the golden age.
  • Battles of Panipat: 1526 (Babur founded Mughal rule), 1556 (Akbar), 1761 (Abdali vs the Marathas). Plassey 1757, Buxar 1764.
  • The Revolt of 1857 began at Meerut. The INC was founded in 1885 by A.O. Hume; its first president was W.C. Bonnerjee.
  • Partition of Bengal 1905 · Muslim League 1906 · Jallianwala Bagh 13 April 1919 · Non-Cooperation 1920 · Chauri Chaura 1922 · Simon Commission 1928 · Dandi March 1930 · Quit India 1942.
  • First Governor-General of Bengal: Warren Hastings. First Governor-General of India: William Bentinck. First Viceroy: Canning. Last Viceroy: Mountbatten. First Indian Governor-General: C. Rajagopalachari.
Geography
  • Largest state by area: Rajasthan. Most populous: Uttar Pradesh. Smallest: Goa.
  • IST is based on 82.5° E (Mirzapur). The Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 states.
  • Highest peak in India: Kangchenjunga. Longest river in India: Ganga. Longest river in the world: Nile. Largest ocean: Pacific.
  • Black soil suits cotton (Deccan). Laterite soil forms in high-rainfall areas.
  • National parks: Jim Corbett (first, 1936, Uttarakhand), Kaziranga (one-horned rhino, Assam), Gir (Asiatic lion, Gujarat), Sundarbans (tiger, mangroves, West Bengal).
  • Palk Strait separates India and Sri Lanka.
  • Planets: Jupiter is the largest, Venus the hottest, Mercury the closest to the Sun, and Mars the "red planet".
Economy
  • RBI was set up in 1935 and nationalized in 1949; its headquarters are in Mumbai. The repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends to banks.
  • The inflation target is CPI 4% ± 2%.
  • NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on 1 Jan 2015. GST began on 1 July 2017.
  • The First Five-Year Plan (1951–56) focused on agriculture. The Second, based on the Mahalanobis model, focused on industry.
  • SEBI became a statutory body in 1992. NABARD was set up in 1982.

11 · EXAM STRATEGY

How to spend the 120 minutes

RoundSectionMinutesRule
1General Awareness10Answer only what you know instantly. Never think longer than 15 seconds.
2Mechanical, first pass55Theory and one-step formula questions. Mark any calculation longer than 90 seconds for review.
3Reasoning35Series, coding, ranking, clock and calendar first. Leave puzzles for last.
4Marked questions20Mechanical numericals first, then reasoning puzzles.

Negative marking, with the numbers

  • A blind guess among 4 options is worth +0.06 marks on average. With one option ruled out it is worth +0.17; with two ruled out, +0.38.
  • Rule: guess only after ruling out at least one option. Keep blind guesses to 10 or fewer, at the very end.
  • Example target: 150 attempted at 85% accuracy gives 127.5 right and 22.5 wrong, so 127.5 − 5.6 ≈ 122 marks.
  • If two options both look right, go back to the trap line for that topic. Examiners build wrong options from those traps.

Skip a question when

  • It needs a steam-table lookup. The table is on screen under "Useful Data", but leave those for round 4.
  • You have spent 90 seconds and still don't see the formula.
  • It is from a Category C topic and is not one line long.

Avoiding calculation mistakes

  • Confirmed A scientific calculator is on screen for the whole exam. Use it for roots, powers like r0.4, logs and long multiplications. Clicking it with the mouse is slow, so do simple arithmetic in your head.
  • Confirmed Rough-work pen and paper are given in the exam lab, and a timer runs on screen.
  • Convert units before anything else: K for temperature, m and N (or mm and N with MPa), radians.
  • Estimate the size of the answer first. Options are often 10× apart (a units error) or 2×/4× apart (a ratio error).
  • Use the rough sheet in columns, one question per box with its number, so you can recheck it.
  • Before you click, read the question's last line again: is it asking for diameter or radius, gauge or absolute, maximum or average?
12 · EXAM-DAY CHECKLIST

Exam-day checklist

  • Printed admit card. Do not write anything on it before the exam, or you will not be let in. Do not fold or damage the barcode.
  • One original photo ID with the same date of birth as the admit card (Aadhaar or e-Aadhaar printout, PAN, voter ID, driving licence, passport, college ID).
  • Reach by 3:45 PM. Entry closes 4:45 PM and no one is let in after that.
  • Allowed: a transparent water bottle. Not allowed: watch of any kind, phone, Bluetooth/earphones, calculator, pen, paper, books. Leave your bag at home if you can; the centre does not store it.
  • Avoid rings, bracelets, chains and other jewellery; there is metal-detector and hand frisking.
  • Face uncovered (no mask). Sit so the webcam sees your full face the whole time; photos are taken during the exam.
  • No toilet break in a 2-hour exam. Eat and use the toilet before you go in.
  • After the first login, write the certification statement in your own running handwriting, sign and give your left thumb impression on the admit card, in front of the invigilator. Also write your mother's name there.
  • At the computer: check the name and photo on screen, find the calculator icon and the "Useful Data" steam table, and read the question palette colours (answered, marked, not answered).
  • In the last 2 minutes, make sure nothing is "marked" without an answer you mean to give. Check how your shift's on-screen instructions treat "marked for review" answers.
  • Get the invigilator to sign your admit card before you leave, and keep a copy of it.
  • Plan your ride home; the exam ends at 7 PM.
